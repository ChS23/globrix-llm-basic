# DOCUMENT 1: COMPREHENSIVE LEGAL GUIDE TO MALAYSIAN REAL ESTATE

## Property ownership in Malaysia combines British colonial law with Malay customary rights

Understanding Malaysian property law requires grasping its unique dual system of land ownership, stringent foreign buyer restrictions that vary dramatically by state, and a robust buyer protection framework that ranks among Asia's strongest. For real estate professionals, mastery of these legal nuances directly impacts transaction success rates and client satisfaction.

The National Land Code 1965 (NLC) governs all land matters in Peninsular Malaysia, establishing a Torrens system where the land register serves as conclusive evidence of ownership. Sabah and Sarawak operate under separate legislation—the Sabah Land Ordinance 1930 and Sarawak Land Code 1958—creating important distinctions that agents must understand when operating across regions.

---

## 1. Property ownership types in Malaysia

### Freehold versus leasehold ownership

**Freehold (Pegangan Bebas)** grants perpetual ownership with no time limitation. Under Section 76(a) of the NLC, when the State Authority disposes land to an individual for an infinite period, it becomes freehold. Owners enjoy complete freedom to sell, transfer, inherit, or mortgage without restrictions, though the government retains compulsory acquisition powers under the Land Acquisition Act 1960.

**Leasehold (Pegangan Pajakan)** grants ownership for a specified period, typically **99 years**, though 60-year and 30-year terms exist. Upon expiry, ownership reverts to the State Authority. The critical threshold is **30 years remaining**—below this, most banks refuse financing, and property values decline significantly.

| Aspect | Freehold | Leasehold |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Price premium | ~20% higher | ~20% lower |
| Bank financing | Standard approval | Difficult below 30 years |
| Transfer process | 3+1 months | May require state consent |
| Appreciation | Faster | Slower after 30 years remaining |

**Lease extension process** involves application to the Land Office using Borang 12A, with premium calculation varying by state. In Selangor, the formula is: Premium = ¼ × 1/100 × Market Value (RM/sq ft) × Years to Renew × Land Area. Selangor offers a 30% rebate for residential properties. Processing takes 6 months to 2 years.

### Malay Reserve Land and Bumiputera lots

**Malay Reserve Land (Tanah Rizab Melayu)** under the Malay Reservation Enactment 1913 is permanently restricted to Malay Muslim ownership only. It cannot be sold, transferred, or rented to non-Malays under any circumstances—even Sabahans, Sarawakians, and non-Malay Muslims are excluded.

**Bumiputera Lots (Lot Bumiputera)** arise from the New Economic Policy 1971 and reserve a percentage of units for Bumiputera buyers (Malays, Orang Asli, Sabah/Sarawak natives). Unlike Malay Reserve Land, Bumi Lots can potentially be released to non-Bumiputeras through state approval and payment of a levy equal to the Bumi discount (typically 5-15%). However, most applications are rejected.

| State | Bumi Quota |
|-------|------------|
| Kuala Lumpur, Selangor, Penang | 30% |
| Terengganu, Kedah, Perlis | Up to 50% |
| Melaka | Up to 60% (outside certain areas) |
| Johor | Tiered: 40% (<RM200K), 30% (RM200K-300K), 20% (>RM300K) |

### Strata titles versus individual titles

**Individual titles** apply to landed properties (bungalows, semi-detached, terrace houses). Each title covers one building lot including land and building, with the owner having complete control subject to local council regulations.

**Strata titles** under the Strata Titles Act 1985 apply to high-rise developments, townhouses, and gated communities with shared facilities. They grant individual unit ownership with shared common property (pools, gyms, corridors). The 2013 amendment mandates strata titles be issued upon Vacant Possession, not six months after CCC as previously.

**Master title** situations arise during development when a single title remains registered under the developer's name. Properties are transferred via Deed of Assignment until individual/strata titles are issued. Buyers face additional risks if the developer enters liquidation.

### Native Customary Rights land in East Malaysia

In **Sarawak**, Native Customary Rights (NCR) land covers approximately 20% of state land, though only ~2% is surveyed and titled. Under Section 5(2) of the Sarawak Land Code 1958, NCR can be established through pre-1958 occupation, cultivation, or burial ground use. NCR land can only be sold to other Sarawak natives.

In **Sabah**, NCR is governed by the Sabah Land Ordinance 1930. Country Land leases were traditionally 999 years; some were converted to 99 years in the 1990s. In 2020, the Sabah government restored 5,878 country lease titles to 999-year tenure.

## 2. Foreign ownership rules and restrictions

### Federal restrictions and prohibited property types

The federal government sets a general minimum threshold of **RM1 million** for foreign purchases, though states can impose higher limits. Foreigners are categorically prohibited from purchasing:

- Malay Reserve Land
- Bumiputera-reserved lots
- Low-cost and medium-low-cost housing
- Agricultural land (most states)
- Properties below the state minimum threshold

| **Selangor (Zone 1-2)** | RM2,000,000 | RM2,000,000 | Only strata-titled landed in gated communities |
| **Selangor (Zone 3)** | RM1,000,000 | RM1,000,000 | Foreigners cannot purchase auction properties |
| **Penang (Island)** | RM1,800,000 (overhang) | RM800,000 (overhang) | 3% state levy applies |

**State levies for foreign purchases**: Penang charges **3%** of purchase price, while Johor and Melaka charge **2%** (Johor minimum RM20,000).

### Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) program

The revised MM2H program (June 2024) now operates on a three-tier system with mandatory property purchase:

| Tier | Fixed Deposit | Property Purchase | Visa Duration |
|------|--------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Silver** | USD 150,000 | >RM600,000 | 5 years |
| **Gold** | USD 500,000 | >RM1,000,000 | 15 years |
| **Platinum** | USD 1,000,000 | >RM2,000,000 | 20 years |

Key 2024 changes include lowered age requirement (25 years), mandatory property purchase (10-year hold minimum), and reduced stay requirement (60 days/year). The MM2H-SEZ category for Forest City only requires USD 32,000-65,000 fixed deposit with no minimum property price.

**Sarawak's S-MM2H** operates separately with RM500,000 fixed deposit, RM600,000 minimum property purchase, and 30 days/year stay requirement.

### Corporate ownership and trust arrangements

Foreign-owned Malaysian companies (Sdn. Bhd.) can purchase property above minimum thresholds. A company is considered "foreign interest" if non-citizens hold more than 50% of voting shares. The minimum paid-up capital is RM250,000 for foreign-interest companies.

**Trust and nominee arrangements** to circumvent foreign ownership restrictions are **illegal and unenforceable**. Malaysian courts will not enforce arrangements designed to evade Bumiputera requirements—property "lies where it falls" if illegality is proven. Foreign buyers should purchase directly in their own name.

### State consent process

Under Section 433B NLC, all foreigners must obtain State Authority consent before registration. Processing takes 1-6 months depending on state. Required documents include:
- Completed State Consent Application Form
- Certified passport copy
- Stamped Sale and Purchase Agreement
- Property title search
- Source of funds documentation (AML compliance)

## 3. Purchase process and procedures

### Primary market (developer sales) step-by-step

**Step 1: Booking** — Pay 2-3% booking fee upon signing Letter of Offer

**Step 2: SPA signing** — Must occur within **21 days** of Letter of Offer. Pay balance to complete 10% deposit.

**Step 3: Loan application** — Submit to multiple banks; approval typically 1-2 weeks

**Step 4: Progressive payments** — Pay in stages as construction progresses:
- 10% at SPA → 10% foundation → 15% framework → 10% walls → 10% roofing → 10% drainage → 10% wiring/plumbing → 10% fixtures → 10% utilities → 5% at VP

**Step 5: Vacant Possession (VP)** — Developer issues notice; buyer has 14 days to collect keys. CCC must be issued first.

**Step 6: Defect inspection** — Document all defects during 24-month DLP

**Step 7: Title transfer** — Upon individual/strata title issuance, Form 14A executed and registered

### Schedule G versus Schedule H SPAs

The Housing Development (Control & Licensing) Regulations 1989 mandate standard SPAs:

| Schedule | Property Type | Delivery Timeline |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Schedule G** | Landed properties | 24 months from SPA |
| **Schedule H** | Strata properties | 36 months from SPA |
| **Schedule I** | Landed (Build-then-Sell) | Upon completion |
| **Schedule J** | Strata (Build-then-Sell) | Upon completion |

### Secondary/subsale market process

**Timeline**: Standard **3+1 months** (3 months completion + 1 month extension). Foreign buyers requiring state consent should expect 5-6 months.

1. Sign Letter of Offer with **2-3% earnest deposit**
2. Appoint lawyer; apply for loan
3. Pay balance deposit (total 10%)
4. Sign SPA (not standard form—terms negotiable)
5. Lodge private caveat (Form 19B) to protect interest
6. Complete discharge of seller's existing loan
7. Execute MOT (Form 14A) upon completion

### Auction properties

Two types exist: **LACA (non-judicial)** for properties without title (5% deposit, 90-day balance) and **Non-LACA (judicial)** for titled properties (10% deposit, 120-day balance).

**Critical risks**: Sold "as is where is," no interior inspection possible, potential existing occupants requiring eviction (RM1,500-2,000 plus court order), and private caveats requiring 2-3 months for removal.

## 4. Taxes and duties

### Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT) — 2024/2025 rates

| Holding Period | Citizens & PRs | Malaysian Companies | Foreigners |
|----------------|---------------|---------------------|------------|
| Years 1-3 | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| Year 4 | 20% | 20% | 30% |
| Year 5 | 15% | 15% | 30% |
| Year 6+ | **0%** | 10% | 10% |

**Exemptions**: Once-in-lifetime exemption for citizens (first private residence), automatic exemption of RM10,000 or 10% of gain (whichever higher), and family transfers (exempt).

**2025 change**: Self-Assessment System (STS RPGT) effective January 1, 2025—mandatory online filing via MyTax within 60 days of disposal.

### Stamp duty rates

**Memorandum of Transfer (MOT)**:
| Property Value | Rate |
|----------------|------|
| First RM100,000 | 1% |
| RM100,001-500,000 | 2% |
| RM500,001-1,000,000 | 3% |
| Above RM1,000,000 | 4% |

**Foreigners**: Flat **4%** on all transfers (effective January 1, 2024)

**Loan agreements**: 0.5% of loan amount

**First-time buyer exemptions** (until December 31, 2025): 100% exemption on MOT and loan stamp duty for properties up to RM500,000. Must be Malaysian citizen, first residential property, never owned/inherited property.

### Recurring property taxes

**Assessment Tax (Cukai Taksiran)**: Collected by local council, typically 2-7% of Annual Rental Value, paid twice yearly (January-February and July-August). Late penalty: 1% per month.

**Quit Rent (Cukai Tanah)**: Collected by State Land Office, due by May 31 annually. Late penalty: 20% of outstanding (minimum RM10). Non-payment may result in land forfeiture.

## 6. Regulatory framework

### Housing Development Act 1966 (HDA)

The HDA protects purchasers of residential properties under construction from licensed developers. Key provisions:

- **Developer licensing**: 3% deposit of estimated construction costs required
- **Housing Development Account (HDA)**: Mandatory account for all buyer payments; withdrawals require architect/engineer certification
- **Defect Liability Period**: 24 months from VP; developer must repair defects within 30 days of written notice
- **Abandoned projects**: Developer abandoning development faces minimum RM250,000 fine or 5 years imprisonment

**Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims**: Exclusive jurisdiction for HDA properties, claims up to RM50,000, RM10 filing fee, decision within 60 days. Must file within 12 months of CCC issuance, DLP expiry, or SPA termination.

### Strata Management Act 2013 (SMA)

Governs strata property management through:
- **Joint Management Body (JMB)**: Formed at first AGM (within 12 months of VP), comprises developer + purchasers
- **Management Corporation (MC)**: Formed upon strata title issuance, comprises all parcel owners
- **Commissioner of Buildings (COB)**: Enforcement authority

### National Land Code 1965

Establishes the Torrens system where the register is conclusive evidence of ownership. Key sections:
- Section 89: Register conclusive evidence
- Section 92(1): Indefeasibility of final title
- Section 340: Indefeasibility subject to exceptions
- Section 433B: State consent required for foreign acquisition

## 8. Common legal issues and dispute resolution

### Caveat lodging

**Private Caveat (Section 323)**: Protects purchaser's interest, lodged using Form 19B, lasts 6 years. Prevents seller from selling to another party.

**Removal methods**: Withdrawal by caveator (Form 19G), application by landowner (Form 19H), court order, or automatic lapse.

### Liquidated Ascertained Damages (LAD)

Rate: **10% per annum** of purchase price, calculated day-to-day from expected VP date. Per Federal Court ruling, calculation starts from booking fee payment date.

**Example**: RM500,000 property, 180-day delay = RM500,000 × 10% × (180/365) = **RM24,657.53**

### Abandoned projects

Under Section 8A HDA, if work stops for 6+ months continuously, buyers can terminate SPA and receive full refund within 30 days (interest-free). Criminal penalty on developer: RM250,000-500,000 fine or 3 years imprisonment.

### Dispute resolution options

| Forum | Jurisdiction | Filing Fee | Timeline |
|-------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims | Up to RM50,000 | RM10 | 60 days |
| Strata Management Tribunal | Strata disputes up to RM50,000 | Similar | Similar |
| Civil Court | Exceeds RM50,000 | Court fees | 6-year limitation |

## 8. Cultural and practical considerations

### Bumiputera policy impacts

**Bumi Quota**: Mandatory allocation for Bumiputera buyers (typically 30%, up to 60% in some states)

**Bumi Discount**: 5-15% discount for Bumiputera buyers (varies by state)

**Bumi Lot vs Malay Reserve Land**: Bumi Lots can potentially be released to non-Bumis with approval and levy payment; Malay Reserve Land cannot be owned by non-Malays under any circumstances.
